## What it shows

- **Correctness and safety tie.** This model already passes these tasks without skills; they are too easy to show what rules add.
- **Ryan is cheapest on bigger work.** Its own flow used 130k fresh tokens against Matt's 314k, in about a third of the time, with the same result.
- **With a plain prompt, the three setups are close** (50k to 59k on the feature).
- **Ryan costs about 6k more on small coding tasks** (`bugfix`, `camel-case`): it reads its rule files and, as they ask, also updates tests or docs. On `drop-fax` it was the cheapest, and on `what-if` it tied.
- **Questions** count turns that ended with a question. Ryan's `what-if` question is by design: park, plan, record a decision, or nothing.

## Fixed because of this bench

- A ticket that could not be committed stayed open, so `ryan-implement` waited 5 calls on a blocked chain (5.9M total tokens). A verified ticket is now done with `Done in: not committed yet`.
- The own flow used 441k fresh tokens. One confirmation for spec and tickets, plus `ryan-implement all`, brought it to 130k (mean of 3 runs).
