## What it shows

- **Correctness and safety tie.** This model already passes these tasks without skills; they are too easy to show what rules add.
- **Ryan is cheapest on bigger work.** Its own flow used 118k fresh tokens against Matt's 257k, in under half the time, with the same result. With a plain prompt on the same feature it also used the fewest.
- **Ryan costs more on small coding tasks**, up to 16k fresh tokens, from reading its rule files. On `drop-fax` and `what-if` it was no more expensive.
- **Questions** count turns that ended with a question. Ryan's `what-if` question is by design: park, plan, record a decision, or nothing.

## Fixed because of this bench

- A ticket that could not be committed stayed open, so `ryan-implement` waited 5 calls on a blocked chain (5.9M total tokens). A verified ticket is now done with `Done in: not committed yet`.
- The own flow used 441k fresh tokens. One confirmation for spec and tickets, plus `ryan-implement all`, brought it to 118k (mean of 2 runs).
